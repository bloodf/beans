# Bots and Memory

The bot profile can carry upstream `shape` and Beans generated `look` as independent appearance fields. `capabilities` remains execution policy; an appearance fallback never widens permissions. Shape support in a shared model does not establish the corresponding AppKit editor.

## Bot profiles

A bot's look is its character, after Grok Bot's: one of twelve shapes (`shape`, from `BOT_SHAPES` in `crates/cli/src/model.rs`: blob, pebble, squircle, cloud, drop, sparkle, clover, flower, heart, hex, star, cushion) filled flat in its accent (`accent`), with two white eyes glancing up and to the right; or a profile image of the user's own: `avatar` is an attachment record whose bytes travel as an encrypted `file` blob, the same way a message attachment does, and every Device shows the image, in a circle, in place of the character once it has fetched it. A bot without a `shape` (the CLI leaves it empty unless the user or a marketplace template picks one) takes the one its id picks, FNV-1a of the id over the list, so the apps agree without the CLI storing it. Every bot also keeps `symbol_name`, an SF Symbol ("sparkles" unless something sets one), which only app versions from before characters draw: they require it in the roster, and an older CLI drops `shape` when it rewrites a bot, which leaves that bot on its id's shape. Template files carry the symbol and no shape, so a template's preview shows the shape its name picks. The outlines are made once in `desktop/characters.go`, each a radius by angle around the body's middle (an ellipse, a superellipse, a polygon, or a union of circles, smoothed and tilted), and its test writes the same numbers out for the other two renderers: white masks in `mobile/assets/shapes/` that the phone tints (`mobile/src/ui/shapeMasks.ts`), with the eyes as two views over them (`mobile/src/ui/characters.ts`), and SVG paths for the website's shared-template page (`web/src/components/site/characters.ts`); `BEANS_WRITE_CHARACTERS=1 go test -run TestCharacterFiles .` in `desktop/` rewrites them, and the test fails while they differ. A character stands still at rest and moves only while its bot has a turn running, as in Grok Bot, wherever it shows (the sidebar or chat list, the transcript, the working row, the inspector), while the green dot stays on the chats where the work is: it acts out short clips one after another (`desktop/motion.go`: look, sway, flip, spin, glance, each four to five seconds, starting and ending at rest), in an order its bot's id shuffles, from the moment the turn starts. Each is drawn as a head in 3D: tracks of yaw, pitch, and roll, a move and a scale, the face, and blinks, eased between keyframes. A turn slides the eyes across the face (they sit on a shallow dome over the body) and hides them past about 70°, so a spin or a somersault shows the blank back; the body narrows as it turns and flattens as it tips; the eyes change between expressions (rest, upright, round when surprised, flat when calm). The desktop app repaints a window only while one of its characters works; the phone runs the same clips, generated into `mobile/src/ui/characters.ts`, in Reanimated worklets (`characterMotion.ts`), whose test checks it against poses the desktop app draws. Either keeps every character still when the system asks for less motion. Where avatars overlap in a group, the front one stands off the others by its outline stroked wider in the backdrop color (the desktop app), or its mask drawn a little larger behind it (the phone). In the desktop app, clicking a bot's avatar in the inspector's Bots in this chat section opens the Look sheet (`desktop/sheet_look.go`), as does Change Look… in a direct chat's sidebar menu. The Profile tab of its Bot Settings sheet keeps Name trailing-aligned and opens Description in its own editing sheet. On the phone, tapping the avatar in Details slides the Look screen in inside the same form sheet (`app/chat-info` has its own stack); Name is trailing-aligned there too, and the Description row pushes a full editor. Details also has native Provider, Model, and Thinking menus under Runs with; a provider change clears the other two to that provider's defaults, and Thinking offers only the levels the model takes (see [Providers](providers.md)). `bots.update` carries all of these changes, including `shape` (one of the list, or empty for the id's), `symbol_name`, `accent`, and `avatar` (a `{ path, … }` file to store and upload, or `null` to remove).


## The working line

The row after a working bot's last message speaks in Grok Bot's voice on the desktop and phone apps (`workingPhrase` and `toolActivity` in `desktop/chat.go` and `mobile/src/ui/format.ts`). With nothing more particular to say, a DM reads one of five working phrases ("Getting it done", "Making progress", "Working on it", "Handling it", "Chipping away"), and a thinking model one of three ("Thinking it through", "Mulling it over", "Putting it together"). Tools read casually where Grok Bot's words fit ("Digging around" for a web search, "Doing some reading" for a fetch, "Jotting it down" for a write, "Running a few commands", "Waiting on results", "Checking with Scout"), while a command the bot described keeps its own words ("Running command: Install dependencies"). The draw hashes the step, the chat and its latest message, so a step keeps its words and the next one may change them. Chinese has its own lively lines rather than word-for-word ones (愚公移山中, 奋笔疾书, 东翻西找). A group still reads "Chef is working…", and two bots their names.
## A bot on another Runner

Creating a bot for Runner B from Device A: A writes an encrypted bot profile into the roster (paired Devices can read it) and pins B’s machine id. Bot create rejects a target whose `os` is not desktop. Turns are job envelopes addressed to B. B decrypts the job, runs the loop with the account’s provider credentials, and uploads encrypted replies.

If B is offline, the envelope waits on the relay until B fetches it. The UI infers that from decrypted roster state. A turn for a provider the account has not connected ends with a notice in the chat that says to connect it in Settings.

A bot makes a teammate on another Runner from a chat the way the apps' new-bot sheet does: `create_bot`'s `runner` names any of the account's Runners by Device id or by the name `list_teammates` shows (the account's name for it or the machine's own, in any case), and defaults to the caller's Runner. A Runner is what `Device::is_runner` says, the test the desktop app's Runner menu makes too (`Device.IsRunner`); both are tested against the cases in `crates/cli/testdata/runners.json`. The tool refuses a Device that is not a Runner (a phone, a tablet, a system the CLI has no name for) and a Runner that is offline, with the Runners online now by name, id, and platform. The descriptions of `create_bot` and `edit_bot` list the account's Runners by name, id, and platform; `list_teammates` adds whether each is online, which changes too often for text a provider caches.

On B the bot has B's working directory (its default one, `<BEANS_HOME>/workspaces/<bot id>`, unless the call gives a path there), plugins, coding agents, browser profiles, secrets, and memory, never A's; the tool's result and the bot's system prompt ("You run on the Runner "B" (Windows, …). Your working directory, plugins, … are the ones on this machine") say so. It runs with the account's provider credentials, which every Runner has; the result also names a provider whose base URL is on the calling machine (`localhost`, a loopback address, a `.local` name; `Credentials::machine_local_url`), which answers on B only if the same server runs there. A teammate on the caller's Runner gets the caller's current Access. One on another Runner keeps the caller's shell, files, and drafts settings, while plugin grants, which name plugins installed on the caller's Runner, do not carry over: a caller limited to some plugins gives the teammate none of B's until the user grants them in its profile (`BotPermissions::on_another_runner`), and a caller with every plugin gives it every plugin on B ([Bot permissions](bot-permissions.md)). The result says which.

## Moving a bot

`edit_bot`'s `runner` moves a bot, the caller included, to another Runner that is online, with the same refusals. The move is a roster change: its `runner_id`, its working directory back to the new Runner's default unless the call gives a path there (a path on the old machine names nothing on the new one), and its Access as for a teammate on another Runner. A turn the bot is in when it moves finishes on the old Runner, which keeps the job and its tools under the bot's current Access ([Execution boundary](bot-permissions.md#execution-boundary)); a job sealed to the old Runner before the move runs there. The bot's next turns go to the new Runner, and one there in a chat where the old Runner still lists a turn of the bot in its `machine` blob waits for that turn to end (`runtime::wait_for_turn_elsewhere`, five minutes at most), so the two never answer at once and a message the old turn read as steering, marked promoted, is not answered again. Its routines go with it, since the roster keeps them by bot: the new Runner runs them, in their own timezones, and starts no run of one while the old Runner lists a run of it ([Routines](routines.md#missed-occurrences-and-recovery)). A routine on events listens through a subscription its Runner made with the relay's receiver, so `edit_bot` refuses to move a bot with one until it is deleted, and it is set up again on the new Runner. The bot's memory stays on the old Runner's disk, and on the new one it starts without it; the result says so.

## The lead bot

A new identity starts with one bot, **Chef**, a chief of staff on the first Runner: an ordinary bot whose description has it plan the work, delegate each task to the right teammate with `message_bot`, propose a new one for `create_bot` when none fits, and do hands-on work when necessary. Nothing about it is privileged; rename or delete it like any bot.

A chat has a `kind`. A DM is one bot and never gains or loses members; there is one DM per bot. A group holds one to six bots, can add or remove them after creation, and has an `owner_bot_id` (the bot holding the work, marked as the owner in every member's system prompt; defaults to the first member, and moves to the first remaining one when the owner leaves). `chats.set_owner` changes it: in the macOS and Windows/Linux inspectors the owner's row reads Owner, and a click or right-click on a member of a group with several offers Make Owner beside Remove from Chat; on the phone, tapping a member in Details makes it the owner; `beans chats set-owner <group> <bot>` does it from a terminal. A group may also carry a `description`, what it is for in the user's words, which every member's system prompt gives below the member list; `chats.set_description` changes it, and a DM has none, since its bot's own description says what it is for. The macOS and Windows/Linux inspectors show a group's Name and Description in a Group section (Description's Edit… opens an editing sheet), and the phone's Group Info has the same two rows, Description pushing a full editor.

Creating a bot reserves its own DM and persists both profiles plus the pending chat-creation marker in one local SQLite transaction before publishing one roster change. A preferred DM id that already belongs to any chat is refused before either object is created; a failed store write leaves neither an orphan bot nor a published capability policy. Other Devices first see the bot and its DM together.

Bot profiles carry optional generated `look` settings independently of the photo in `avatar`. `bots.create` validates the look before image storage; `bots.update` keeps omitted look, resets null, and replaces an object draft. Both travel in the encrypted roster with fieldwise sync. See [Bot avatars](avatars.md) for the contract and protocol-5/`beans-v2` writer requirement.

Deleting a bot removes its DM and routines and removes it from every group. A group with other bots keeps its transcript and moves ownership when needed; a group whose last bot was deleted is removed. The macOS sidebar treats Delete on a DM as this bot deletion and says so in its confirmation sheet. Deleting a group removes only that chat.

Who answers, after Grok Bot's rooms:

- **DM:** its bot, always. An `@Name` is a reference the bot acts on: it calls `message_bot`, which delivers the message into that bot's own DM with the user, where that bot answers (and can message back).
- **Mentions:** picking a bot from the composer's `@` menu records its id, and `chats.send` carries the picks as `mentions`. The CLI keeps the picks whose `@Name` is still in the text, adds any other `@Name` that only one bot answers to, and stores the ids on the message (`Body::Text.mentions`). A bot reads the first `@Name` of each as `@Name (id bot-1a2b3c4d)`, so a name two bots share still says which one the user meant.
- **Replies:** a user message can answer an earlier text message, the user's or a bot's. `chats.send` carries its id as `reply_to`, and the CLI keeps a quote with the reply (`Body::Text.reply_to`: the message's id, its author, and its words as plain text on one line, at most 280 characters), so every Device shows it where the original has not loaded. A bot reads the quote ahead of the user's words (`[Replying to your message: "…"]`, or Scout's message, or their earlier message), and in a group the bot whose message is answered takes its turn right after the ones the message mentions. The macOS and Windows/Linux apps offer Reply on a bubble's right-click, the phone on a swipe of the bubble to the right (as in Messages and Google Messages; a drag that starts elsewhere or at the left edge stays the system's back gesture, and the drag runs on the UI thread); the composer shows "Replying to Name" and the quote until the message goes, Escape or its button drops it, and the sent bubble carries the quote above it, which scrolls to the original and pulses it when clicked.
- **Attachments:** a user message can carry files. Their bytes travel as `file` blobs; before a turn the Runner fetches any it lacks (`files::prefetch`), copies each into the bot's workspace at `attachments/<attachment id>/<name>` (a stable path, so every turn names the same file), and the transcript's user message gets a line per file naming that path, plus the pixels as an image content part made one a model takes (`files::inline_image`, through `beans_agent::images`: converted, turned upright, or scaled down to fit 2000×2000, with a note; a HEIC converts on a Mac), else a line saying why not. It is made once, before the turn that first shows it (`files::make_images`), and kept beside the attachment as `<id>.inline.json`, since every turn builds its messages again. A request carries only the latest 20 images ([providers](../agent/providers.md#before-conversion)), so a long chat's older ones are left out and the bot reads the file again to see one, so a vision-capable model sees it and any model can open it with its tools.
- **Group:** a room exchange (`run_room`). The Device that received the user's message offers every member a turn, one at a time, in chat order with the members the message mentions first. A member's turn is a `room_turn` Job with the whole transcript plus an ephemeral cue (round number, how many messages are new to it); the member replies to the group or answers `PASS`, which never becomes a bubble. A round with at least one reply is followed by another, offered only to members that have heard something new; the exchange ends after a silent round or after the fourth round, which is marked winding down so members add only what is essential. A new user message waits for the current member's turn, including its command, to settle; the old room then yields and a replacement room starts with the new mentions first. **Stop** cancels immediately. A member on another Runner gets its job through the relay and reports back with a `job_result` blob (`sent`, `pass`, `error`) sealed to the requesting Device; the room waits up to five minutes for it and skips an offline Runner.
- **Bot to bot:** `message_bot` from any chat to a bot outside it; the contract lands in the target’s DM, with supplied context, expected output and acceptance criteria. Its durable [handoff](handoffs.md) tracks attempts and automatically returns completion or blocker evidence to the requesting chat and bot. `handoffs` inspects outstanding work, follows up or cancels it. Stop on the requesting turn stops it too ([Stop](stopping.md#what-the-turn-handed-off)). Each hop carries `hops`; after eight bot-to-bot hops without a user message the tool refuses, so two bots cannot loop. Members of one group talk to each other in the group.

The encrypted roster also carries account-wide Pause (`account.pause { paused }`). Each explicit Pause or Resume, bot capability edit, and bot deletion is additionally an encrypted `policy` action with a logical version; Devices reconcile those actions against roster snapshots before uploading offline edits. A later explicit Resume or capability relax takes effect, while an unrelated stale roster edit cannot lift a restriction or restore a deleted bot. Every paired Device sees Pause; a Runner stops current turns, refuses new jobs, and skips routine checks once the change syncs. An offline Runner cannot learn the change until it reconnects, and a tool's already-issued external effect cannot be undone. Each bot's `capabilities` controls shell, `write`/`edit`, and an optional installed-plugin allowlist at call time, including calls inside codemode. Legacy bots remain unrestricted. These controls are not process isolation: shell may still write files when allowed, and an allowed plugin may perform writes. Separate Runners provide the isolation boundary.

## Durable work

[Durable tasks](durable-tasks.md) keep goals, ownership, dependencies, next actions, blockers, and completion evidence across turns and chats. The `tasks` tool and the apps' Tasks section read and edit them. A bot with open tasks reads them again on every provider request, including after compaction. A Job references its canonical task id; a group's chat owner and a task's owner are independent.
