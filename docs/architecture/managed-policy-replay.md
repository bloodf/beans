# Managed policy receipts and replay

See [Protocols](protocols.md#cli--relay) for the account transport and blob protocol.

The relay exposes Store primitives for opaque managed policy/roster ciphertext committed together, with frozen version-1 receipt lookup before CAS, checked SQLite FULL and transaction-local Postgres `synchronous_commit=on`, atomic quota/sequence/receipt changes, exact historical retries and separate transaction/blob namespaces. `managed_companion_receipts` survives ordinary roster/blob/group deletion; generic insert/precheck refuses retained blob-ID reuse. History is independently capped at 65536 receipts and 64 MiB charged retained bytes, each receipt at 1024 bytes and combined base64url companion ciphertext at 32768 bytes; true account teardown removes history, replay incarnation and usage with either revoke value. Staged replay starts with empty context/token, through=0 and since=0, captures committed head and ordinary-plus-managed policy linkage in one SQLite read snapshot or Postgres read-only REPEATABLE READ snapshot, and returns one complete policy envelope per page with lookahead. HMAC tokens bind account, durable account incarnation, boot session, cut, expiry, cursor and cumulative bounds; forged/split cuts, changed contexts/cursors, expiry and teardown/reset reject. Tokens expire after 300 seconds and enforce at most 65537 pages, 1048576 scanned policy envelopes and 1 GiB policy ciphertext; page serialization is bounded at 8 MiB. Managed pages verify immutable request/hash/paired-sequence linkage and advance past the paired roster sequence. Ordinary policies remain a distinct opaque subtype and are unslotted/unaddressed/ungrouped; backend deletion predicates preserve policy history. Replay tokens do not establish plaintext consent or client readiness: consumers still validate/decrypt/persist and align latest roster correspondence. The opt-in authenticated blobs transport below exposes replay and receipts; protocol-6 activation remains blocked.

## Authenticated blobs transport

`GET /v1/blobs?policy_replay=1&since=0` captures the first cut; `kinds=policy` and `limit=1..1024` are optional. Continuations supply all of `context`, returned `cut_token`, `through` and `since=next_since`; carry each newly returned token with its matching cursor. Unknown or duplicate replay query fields, incomplete continuations and oversized identifiers/tokens reject. The response is the closed version-1 cut DTO with `schema_version`, `context`, `cut_token`, `through`, `next_since`, `done`, `blobs`; opaque `subtype=ordinary` and `subtype=managed` records retain the existing Store DTO, including byte-array ciphertext and immutable managed receipts. Responses are at most 8 MiB. The current Store returns one complete envelope even if the requested upper limit is larger.

`GET /v1/blobs?policy_replay=1&receipt_transaction_id=<transaction>` returns the closed immutable receipt, or 404 for an unknown transaction in the caller's account. Receipt lookup does not establish current replay context or grant eligibility; it is historical commitment evidence. Context/cursor/limit parameters cannot accompany receipt lookup. Existing capability middleware and bearer rate limits apply; account identity comes only from the authenticated bearer, and current paired membership is checked before and after storage awaits. Cross-account tokens and stale replay contexts reject. Generic `GET /v1/blobs` retains its existing response and paging semantics.

The CLI first-sync and held-work control pulls inspect the optional replay transport before their existing legacy blob processing. An initial successful response matching the closed ordinary `{blobs,seq}` policy-page contract establishes legacy-only transport: required fields, ordered nonnegative sequences, unaddressed policy records, bounded IDs and valid bounded base64url ciphertext are checked before legacy processing resumes. This outcome is distinct from complete managed inspection and supplies zero managed authority. Unknown bodies, decoder failures and HTTP errors are not absence evidence. Once a managed cut is recognized, continuations and receipt lookups accept only their strict managed DTOs; a later ordinary page cannot downgrade the session. The dedicated reader caps each streamed response at 8 MiB before JSON decoding, rejects unknown/duplicate/missing fields and unsupported subtypes/versions, pins context and through across continuations, validates progress and complete paired sequences, and checks ciphertext lengths, SHA-256 hashes, request commitments and exact account-bound immutable receipt lookup. It retains only transient identity sets and continuation state, bounded to 65537 pages, 1 GiB policy ciphertext and a 300-second deadline; ciphertext is discarded after inspection. Refused, partial, mismatched or stale managed cuts stop before those callers apply blobs or move cursors; authentication, revocation, rate-limit, server, storage and transport failures propagate without fallback. Valid inspection resumes unchanged legacy policy processing but does not decrypt managed consent, persist readiness, advance an authoritative managed cursor, enable grants or deliver provider resources. Receipt and context validation depends on the authenticated relay's completeness; the CLI does not authenticate opaque HMAC tokens independently or prove current plaintext roster correspondence.
