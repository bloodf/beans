# Relay protocol

## CLI ↔ relay

- Account requests, including registration, challenge/verify, pairing mailboxes and the sync socket upgrade, require exactly one `Beans-Protocol: 5` and one `Beans-Format: beans-v2` capability. Missing, malformed or duplicated capabilities fail closed before account effects. `User-Agent: beans/<version> (<os>)` belongs to the relay client, never a provider. Protocol 5 is the incompatible fresh Beans boundary; it retains paging, attestation, durable policy actions, roster CAS and appearance preservation. Health advertises `format: "beans-v2"`, `protocol`, effective `min_protocol` and `min_roster_protocol`, both at least 5 regardless of an operator lowering the setting. Clients reject missing, malformed, wrong-format or unsupported evidence before sync or pairing. Older account requests receive `426`; there is no old-protocol account-route exception. Public health and plaintext catalogs remain separate from account routes.
- Health additionally advertises `memory_config_version: 1`. The separate encrypted memory-config blob remains schema 1 under protocol 5/format `beans-v2`; it is not part of credentials or roster. Cores fail account sync closed when capability evidence is absent, malformed or unsupported. `memory_config` uses account DEK/kind AAD and the latest-only slot, per-record logical merge and durable tombstones. Local config and encrypted outbox commit atomically.
- The macOS app shows a protocol-update sheet once per launch and reports incompatibility in its Device pane; the Beans phone shows "Update Beans to sync" in the chat list and Settings.
- `relay_update_required` appears in hello, snapshots and relay status. Incompatible sync retries every fifteen minutes and clears on a successful connection or relay URL change; paired Devices do not silently proceed on unsupported evidence.
- Machine bearer for blobs, the sync socket, the machine list, and a paired machine attesting another; identity signature for registering the identity and for the machines an identity device attests.
- Bearer caching and registration publication retain local account and relay ownership; see [Identity](identity.md#unpairing-a-device).
- PUT/GET non-file blobs as base64url ciphertext in JSON, and PUT/GET files as binary ciphertext. `roster` is a whole-roster snapshot of bots, chats, and routines; `policy` is an append-only encrypted explicit Pause, bot capability, or bot removal action with a logical version and Device tie-break. Devices reconcile those actions with the latest roster before publishing queued snapshots, so a stale Device cannot undo a newer policy action even when its roster upload races another Device. The relay cannot read policy values. `chat` is one upsert or removal of a message; `machine` is a Device’s metadata and the turns in flight on it; `credentials` is the account's provider credentials; `key` is the DEK sealed to the content key.
- Individual `DELETE /v1/blobs/{id}` protects `roster`, `policy` and `memory_config` for every protocol-admitted client. SQLite and Postgres apply the kind restriction in the atomic DELETE itself, without a lookup/check/delete race. Protected ids return `404` and leave rows, sequence and usage unchanged; file cleanup and consumed-envelope deletion remain available.
- Jobs: `kind=job` with `recipient_machine_pubkey`, sealed to that machine’s box key, deleted by the Runner after the turn.
- Questions: `kind=request` sealed to one Runner (`crates/cli/src/requests.rs`: `{ id, verb, requested_by, body }`), answered with a `kind=response` sealed to the Device that asked (`{ request_id, body, error? }`); each side deletes the blob it consumed, and the asker gives up after 20 s, or 75 s for the start of a plugin sign-in, whose discovery and registration the Runner cuts off at 45 s, or 150 s for `mcp.reconnect`, which answers once a server has connected or failed. The verbs are `memory.read` and `memory.write`, so a bot's memory can be shown and edited from a Device that is not its Runner; `plugins.install` / `plugins.uninstall` / `plugins.variables` / `plugins.connect` / `plugins.sign_out` / `plugins.detail`, so plugins on a Runner are managed from any Device, and `plugins.sign_in.finish` / `plugins.sign_in.cancel`, with which the Device that asked for a sign-in (its `redirect_uri` on `plugins.connect` or `permission.answer`) hands back where its browser landed (see Sign-in in [Codemode and Plugins](plugins.md#plugins)); the `mcp.*` methods but `mcp.parse`, so a Runner's own MCP servers are managed from any Device; `permission.answer`, so a permission card is answered from any Device; `bash.stdin`, `bash.stop`, and `bash.background`, so a command a bot runs is answered, stopped, or sent to the background from any Device; `chats.send_now`, so a message the bot's turn holds is read at once from any Device. A request is refused up front when the Runner is unknown or offline.
- Slots: a `roster`, `chat`, `machine`, or `credentials` blob goes up with a `slot` (`Slot` in `app.rs`, `ChatBlob::slot`), and the relay deletes the blobs of that slot it supersedes, so the log holds a message once and not once per streamed chunk. A message's versions and its removal share the slot named by the message id. An upsert sets `keep_first`, which spares the slot's oldest blob: its seq holds the message's place in the log, so a Device that replays from the start gets the transcript in order, first the early version and later the final one. A tool row is the exception: its running version goes up without arguments or results, for the status line on other Devices, and the finished row supersedes it outright, so the relay keeps one version of each call, at the finished row's place. A row that shows a terminal keeps its first place as a message does, since the transcript shows it. A removal supersedes every version. The SQLite outbox does the same before upload: a queued version updates its row without moving its queue position. `App::upsert_message` holds one lock from the SQLite write that gives a message its `position` to the enqueue, so the sending Device's transcript order is the log's order.
- Groups: a `chat` blob and a message's `file` blob go up with `group`, the chat's id (`ChatBlob::group`). Deleting a chat queues its id in `State::group_deletes`, drops what the outbox still held for it, and the sync cycle calls `DELETE /v1/groups/{group}` until it lands: the relay deletes the group's rows and the attachments' objects in one transaction, gives the bytes back to the quota, and remembers the group in `deleted_groups`, so a blob that names it later (a Runner finishing a turn in a chat another Device deleted) gets `409` and the outbox drops it. The other Devices learn of the deletion from the roster. A bot's avatar is a `file` with no group: the Device that replaces or removes it, or deletes its bot, removes its local copy and queues the attachment id in `State::blob_deletes`, and the sync cycle calls `DELETE /v1/blobs/{id}` until it lands, which removes the row and the object.
- `GET /v1/groups/{group}/blobs?before=<place>&limit=` reads a chat backwards a message at a time: `{ slots: [{ place, blobs }], has_more }`, oldest first, where a slot is a message (its first and latest blob, or its removal) or the chat's read mark, and `place` is the slot's lowest seq. A message other than a tool row keeps the place its first version took however late its last one landed, so pages read backwards give the transcript in the log's order. A page is up to 500 slots and 8 MiB, and leaves out the group's `file` blobs. A relay without the route answers `404`, and the Device replays the log.
- `GET /v1/blobs?since=` answers a page of up to 8 MiB of ciphertext (and at least one blob); a Device asks again from the last seq it got. A Device at seq 0 first takes `roster,policy,machine` without chat messages, so chats have their names and bots and policy actions are reconciled before transcript paging.
- Incoming `chat` `ClearUnread` decrypts before a local SQLite transaction. The transaction commits unread state, the incoming blob receipt and, for forward pulls, `last_seq` before live publication. A storage failure stops before that envelope's cursor advancement; retry does not skip it or repeat an accepted read mark. This is per-read admission, not whole-page rollback: earlier operations retain their existing effects, and deferred bulk job results publish after the accepted prefix snapshot. Bulk mode resets on rejection. Initial chat paging does not advance the forward cursor; older history paging ignores historical read marks. Other blob kinds retain their existing page-tail persistence semantics.
- Every Device keeps `last_seq`, the latest observed or accepted roster slot seq, and its outbox in `beans.sqlite3`; queued ciphertext is a binary SQLite BLOB. It also persists the last observed plaintext roster with its seq, and freezes that baseline when it first queues a roster edit, through restarts and retries. A `roster` PUT carries `expected_slot_seq` (0 before the first roster), and the relay atomically checks that value against the current roster slot before replacing it. A mismatch returns `409`; a repeat of the same blob id is accepted. Uploads retry until accepted. Attachment transfers have a ten-minute request timeout.
- Fieldwise roster merge treats bot `look` as a complete replace/reset field. Unrelated local edits preserve the remote look, including retained future keys; a locally changed look replaces it without overwriting remote photo or profile changes. The existing frozen baseline, encrypted outbox and roster CAS carry appearance intent through offline edits and restart.
- Before uploading a queued roster, the Device previews the latest roster slot without applying it, then pulls the roster and policy actions. After the pull it reloads the queued roster, including any policy projection created during first sync, and rebases it over the observed remote roster before upload. It compares the queued snapshot with its frozen baseline to apply only locally changed bot, chat, and routine fields, additions, and explicit removals over the remote roster. Remote unrelated changes and deletions of unchanged entities survive; local edits to an existing group or routine do not disappear. A merged group with zero or more than six members is a conflict: sync keeps local intent, transcript, and queued attachments, leaves the remote roster untouched, reports the group and member count, and does not upload the roster. Resolve group membership on a paired Device, then reconcile the queued local edit before retrying. Policy Pause, capabilities, and bot removals take precedence even during a blocked roster upload. Locally created chats retain their transcripts, queued `chat`/`file` blobs, and creation markers until their exact roster upload succeeds; remote omission of an unchanged existing chat deletes its local transcript and outbox. A relay CAS `409` keeps the queued intent and markers and retries after pulling the winner.
- A locally created chat keeps its chosen id until its queued roster lands. The Device persists its original kind, member bot ids, and owner with the creation marker. Older ID-only markers remain pending, but cannot establish whether a remote chat with the same id is its own accepted creation; sync refuses that ambiguous collision instead of treating a later local membership edit as proof. Each incoming roster application reloads the queue and markers while holding the roster edit lock, and compares original identity against an incoming chat with the same id before changing local state or transcript. A foreign collision stops the pull; local bots, messages, files, and policy remain queued. A matching original identity also recovers a successful PUT whose response was lost: the Device rebases newer local edits onto the accepted roster and acknowledges the creation. Successful PUT acknowledgments clear markers even if a newer local edit replaced the outbox slot during upload; membership and owner changes do not prevent acknowledgment. Edits or deletion of newly published bots, chats, and routines remain in the newer queued roster.
- Before each roster PUT, the Device durably records the exact submitted plaintext snapshot and expected slot seq in its existing baseline table. If a newer local edit replaces the outbox while the response is delayed or lost, recovery extends the common ancestor with creations from that submitted snapshot only. A submitted new routine with the same id and bot id in a later remote slot establishes routine creation even without a pending chat marker. A later remote roster may contain other Devices' new bots, chats, or routines; absence from the local queue is not permission to delete those unrelated creations. Fieldwise merge preserves their changes and later local edits or removals of the submitted creations, including across a local restart. An acknowledged PUT clears the submitted snapshot. A mixed-roster recovery whose older store lacks the original submitted snapshot stops with a roster conflict, retaining local intent instead of guessing ownership.
- A queued roster from a store created before baseline persistence has no evidence to distinguish old state from offline edits. If its observed slot changed, sync detects the conflict in a non-destructive roster preview, leaves its ciphertext, local transcript, file outbox, and creation markers queued, applies current policy events, and reports manual reconciliation instead of uploading stale state. With an unchanged slot, the queued content can upload after current policy is imposed.
- A roster-validation conflict emits disconnected `relay.status` with its `error.message` even when the sync socket successfully upgraded before preview or pull failed. Repeated upgrades and conflict failures report the same cause while retaining queued ciphertext and its frozen baseline and leaving the remote roster unchanged. Ordinary post-connection socket loss remains quiet during retry, and stale bearers retry authentication without an error warning. This status behavior does not change fieldwise merge precedence or roster CAS.

See [Managed policy receipts and replay](managed-policy-replay.md#authenticated-blobs-transport) for opt-in `policy_replay=1` replay and receipt lookup on the authenticated blobs route, alongside staged managed-policy commits and receipt retention. Generic blob paging and protocol floors remain unchanged.

```
App_B → CLI_B
CLI_B encrypts user message + job-for-A → relay
CLI_A fetches envelopes for A, decrypts, runs loop, encrypts reply
CLI_B fetches chat blobs, decrypts → App_B
```
